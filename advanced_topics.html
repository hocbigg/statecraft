Advanced Topics: Statecraft

These specialization tracks build upon the foundations established in the core curriculum. Self-directed learners should select one or two tracks aligned with their specific strategic interests rather than attempting to complete every track simultaneously.

  • Grand Strategy and Strategic Studies — The formulation and execution of long-term national policy, military doctrine, net assessment, and civil-military relations.
  • Regime Dynamics and Comparative Authoritarianism — The institutional mechanics of authoritarian survival, democratic backsliding, populist mobilization, and regime transitions.
  • Geoeconomics and Economic Statecraft — The deployment of economic instruments, including financial sanctions, trade controls, and currency leverage, to achieve geopolitical objectives.
  • Applied Political Theory and Public Ethics — Advanced normative theory applied to state action, covering human rights jurisprudence, comparative justice, minority rights, and public reason.
  • Conflict, Security, and Negotiation — Modern security management, counterterrorism strategy, international negotiation tradecraft, and the micro-dynamics of civil warfare.

Grand Strategy and Strategic Studies

This track covers the alignment of national ends, ways, and means across military planning, competitive assessment, and civilian governance.

Grand strategy formulation: U.S. Army War College Guide to National Security Issues, Vol. I: Theory of War and Strategy, 5th Ed. (Dr. J. Boone Bartholomees Jr., Strategic Studies Institute) - An open military college textbook that details the analytical frameworks professional strategists use to translate political policy into military objectives.

Net assessment methodology: Casting Net Assessment: Andrew W. Marshall and the Epistemic Community of the Cold War (Lt. Col. John M. Schutte, Air University Press) - A monograph analyzing the Pentagon's Office of Net Assessment and its long-range method for evaluating relative military balances and competitor blind spots.

Nuclear deterrence and strategic stability: Living at the Nuclear Brink: Yesterday and Today (William J. Perry and Scott Sagan, Stanford Online/edX) - A free-to-audit course on escalation management, nuclear proliferation, and crisis stability taught by former defense officials and security scholars.

Civil-military relations: Civil-Military Relations, 17.584 (Roger Petersen, MIT OpenCourseWare) - A complete graduate-level syllabus with lecture notes examining civilian control, coups d'état, and military institutional culture across democratic and authoritarian regimes.

Regime Dynamics and Comparative Authoritarianism

This track investigates how political regimes institutionalize power, resist external shocks, and experience democratic erosion or autocratic consolidation.

Democratic erosion and resilience: Countering Illiberalism & Building Democratic Resilience (AUTHLIB / Participate Practically) - An applied course based on European research that equips learners to diagnose illiberal institutional maneuvers and evaluate legal and civic counter-strategies.

Conditions of democratic transitions: Comparative Democratic Development Part I: Conditions of Democracy (Larry Diamond, Stanford Online/edX) - A structured university course evaluating the economic, cultural, and institutional variables that facilitate or impede democratic consolidation.

Contemporary authoritarian shifts: Power and Politics in Today's World (Ian Shapiro, YaleCourses on YouTube) - A complete filmed lecture series detailing post-Cold War political realignments, the resurgence of populist nationalism, and the decay of traditional party systems.

Comparative authoritarian survival: How Dictatorships Work: Power, Personalization, and Collapse (Barbara Geddes, Joseph Wright, and Erica Frantz) - An empirical, data-driven study of ruling coalitions, elite power-sharing, personalization of control, and vulnerability to collapse across autocracies.

Geoeconomics and Economic Statecraft

This track explores the strategic use of asymmetric economic interdependencies, trade policy, and financial leverage as coercive instruments of state policy.

Theoretical foundations of economic coercion: Economic Statecraft: New Edition (David A. Baldwin) - The foundational monograph that develops an analytical framework for evaluating economic statecraft, positive inducements, and international economic pressure.

International finance and monetary mechanics: International Finance (Marginal Revolution University) - A video-based economics course covering exchange rate mechanics, balance-of-payments crises, and currency regimes necessary for analyzing financial warfare.

Sanctions architecture and diplomatic coercion: The Art of Sanctions: A View from the Field (Richard Nephew) - A practitioner's framework on how to design, calibrate, and enforce economic sanctions regimes by measuring economic pain against political resolve.

Institutional political economy and development: Political Economy of Institutions and Development (Universiteit Leiden, Coursera) - A free-to-audit course on how state capacity, domestic institutional design, and governance structures affect economic growth and resource autonomy.

Applied Political Theory and Public Ethics

This track examines advanced normative frameworks, ethical constraints on sovereign authority, and the legal obligations of states under international human rights regimes.

International human rights jurisprudence: International Human Rights Law (Olivier De Schutter, Université Catholique de Louvain / edX) - A legal course analyzing state obligations, enforcement mechanisms, and international treaty protections against state encroachment.

Comparative theories of justice: The Idea of Justice (Amartya Sen) - An advanced treatise contrasting ideal-institutional approaches to justice with realization-focused comparisons aimed at removing actual societal deprivations.

Minority rights and constitutional pluralism: Multicultural Citizenship: A Liberal Theory of Minority Rights (Will Kymlicka) - The seminal text exploring how constitutional states reconcile liberal neutrality with group-differentiated rights and multiethnic stability.

Public reason and post-secular legitimacy: Religion in the Public Sphere (Jürgen Habermas, University of San Diego) - A foundational paper detailing the institutional requirements for translating religious argumentation into publicly justifiable policy in constitutional democracies.

Conflict, Security, and Negotiation

This track provides specialized training in modern defense management, violent non-state actors, cross-border bargaining, and the logic of civil conflicts.

International security management: International Security Management (Erasmus University Rotterdam, Coursera) - An applied course addressing the contemporary security environment, open-source intelligence collection, organized crime networks, and threat mitigation.

Terrorism and counterterrorism strategy: Terrorism and Counterterrorism: Comparing Theory and Practice (Universiteit Leiden, Coursera) - A research-driven course evaluating the historical waves of terrorism, radicalization processes, and the efficacy of domestic and multilateral counterterrorism policies.

International and diplomatic negotiation: International and Cross-Cultural Negotiation (ESSEC Business School, Coursera) - An applied course on diplomatic bargaining that trains learners to navigate cultural negotiating styles, stakeholder interests, and impasse resolution.

Micro-dynamics of internal armed conflict: The Logic of Violence in Civil War (Stathis N. Kalyvas) - The field's standard theoretical and empirical text analyzing how information asymmetries, territorial control, and selective coercion shape violence in irregular civil wars.
